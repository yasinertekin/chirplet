Privacy policy

Last updated 2026-11-18. Contact: yasinertekinn@outlook.com

In short

Chirplet shows no ads and has no account of its own. To learn about purchases, ads, crashes and use, it uses four outside services: Adapty, which tells us how the Premium paywalls are seen and bought (see Purchases), Meta's software, which tells Meta that the app was installed and opened, so that we can see which of our ads on Facebook and Instagram brought people to the app (see Our ads), Firebase Crashlytics, which sends us a report when the app crashes (see Crash reports), and Google Analytics for Firebase, which counts what happens in the app (see Usage statistics). Apple calls that tracking; your advertising identifier is used for it only if you allow it when iOS asks. So that the app works, Apple's own services, Fly.io, which runs our relay, and GitHub, which hosts the moderation list the app downloads, receive data too, and Apple also receives data for Apple Ads attribution (see Purchases); every service that receives data is listed under Who receives your data. We, the developer, collect no other data about you.

A few things do reach us, and each is explained below: what Adapty counts about the Premium paywalls and purchases (see Purchases), what Meta tells us about how many installs and purchases each of our ads brought (see Our ads), a report when the app crashes (see Crash reports), a short record of what happens in the app (see Usage statistics), the short messages the app sends to our push relay so that your partner's and friends' phones can hear about what you do (see The push relay), live play's sealed messages, which the relay passes on and cannot read (see Live play), a web browser's request for the invite page (see Invite links and the App Clip), and an email you choose to send as feedback or a report, and a report someone else sends about you (see Feedback).

What the app keeps

Your pets, their care history, your coins, what you bought in the shop and your settings are kept on this iPhone, where the app and its widgets read them.

When iCloud is on, the same data is also saved in your own iCloud account (Apple's CloudKit, in your private database), so your pets come back on a new iPhone. Only you can read it there; we can't.

The Apple Watch

On an Apple Watch, the Watch app keeps its own copy of your pets on the watch and syncs with your iCloud account itself, the same way the iPhone does (Apple's CloudKit).

Purchases

Premium is bought through the App Store with your Apple Account. We never see your name, email or payment details. To unlock Premium, the app reads your Premium purchase from the App Store on this iPhone and keeps a copy here: the plan, when it was bought, when it ends or renews, whether a family member shares it with you, whether it was refunded, its App Store numbers, and whether it was bought in the App Store or in a test version of the app.

So your other devices unlock it too, your pets keep a note of your Premium plan and when it ends, whether it was bought in the App Store or in a test version of the app, and a code made from the purchase's App Store number, which lets your devices recognise a refund. Like the rest of your data, it is kept only on your devices and in your own iCloud, and on a pet you raise together, the other person can read it too (see below).

To learn which paywall works best, the app uses Adapty (adapty.io), a subscription analytics service that keeps this data for us. When you open the app, it asks Adapty which Premium plans its paywalls show; it tells Adapty when a paywall appears, and when you buy, restore or renew Premium. Adapty receives the App Store's signed record of each Premium purchase (the plan, price, dates, its App Store numbers, and whether it was refunded), which paywall it was bought from, the paywalls you saw, a random number Adapty makes for this installation, the identifier iOS gives our apps on this iPhone (the identifier for vendor), and the device model, iOS version, language, time zone and app version.

Adapty does not receive your name, email, Apple Account or payment details, and nothing about your pets. The app also tells Adapty not to collect your IP address. Adapty receives your advertising identifier only if you allowed tracking when iOS asked, and it passes your Premium purchases on to Meta, so that we can see which ads led to them (see Our ads). Once per installation, Adapty's software also passes on the token iOS gives apps to learn whether they were installed from an ad on the App Store (Apple Ads); Apple does not count this as tracking. Premium itself is still decided on this iPhone from the App Store alone, and it works without Adapty.

Our ads

We advertise Chirplet on Facebook and Instagram. To see which ads work, the app contains Meta's software (the Facebook SDK, from Meta Platforms). When you open the app, it tells Meta that the app was installed and opened, with a random number it makes for this installation, the identifier for vendor, the device model, iOS version, language, time zone and app version, and, as with every request over the internet, your IP address. Adapty tells Meta about Premium purchases and renewals (the plan, the price and the time) with the same random number. Meta never receives your name, email, Apple Account or payment details from the app, and nothing about your pets.

The first time you open the app, iOS asks whether Chirplet may track you. If you allow it, Meta and Adapty also receive your advertising identifier; Meta can then match the install and the purchases to the ad you saw and to your Facebook or Instagram account, and use them for advertising, as Meta's own privacy policy describes. If you do not allow it, the advertising identifier is never read, and Meta counts installs and purchases without it. You can change your answer at any time in the iPhone's Settings, under Privacy and Security, then Tracking. The app itself shows no ads.

Crash reports

When Chirplet crashes, it sends a crash report to Firebase Crashlytics, a Google service, the next time you open it, so that we can find and fix the fault. The report holds where in the app's code the crash happened, the date, the device model and its state at the time (such as its orientation, free memory and disk space), the iOS and app versions, a random number Firebase makes for this installation and, as with every request over the internet, your IP address. It holds nothing about your pets, no name, email, Apple Account or advertising identifier, and it is not used for ads or tracking. Google keeps crash reports for 90 days.

Usage statistics

To learn which parts of Chirplet people use and where they get stuck, the app sends Google Analytics for Firebase, a Google service, a short record when certain things happen in it: an onboarding page reached, onboarding finished or skipped, a paywall shown or closed, a purchase or restore started and how it ended, a care action or mini-game, an egg started, hatched or grown, an item bought in the shop, a Pet Studio form saved, a friend action, and a main screen opened. Each record carries the app version, the device model, iOS version, language, a random number Firebase makes for this installation and, as with every request over the internet, your IP address, from which Google estimates a rough location (country and region). It holds nothing you typed, no pet names, no name, email, Apple Account or advertising identifier, and it is not used for ads or tracking. Google keeps the records for two months and keeps only totals after that.

Raising a pet together

Sharing a pet is up to you: it starts only when you invite one person with a link, and only that person can join. The pet then lives in the owner's iCloud, and Apple's CloudKit shares it with the other person.

The other person can see the pet, its whole care log with the names you each chose ("Ece fed Bit"), its settings and looks, and the Premium note on the pet: the plan, when it ends, and the code made from the purchase's App Store number. That code is made from your purchase, not from your name or Apple Account, but it is the same on every pet you raise, so it is a pseudonym rather than a secret. Your coins stay yours and are never shared. Your shop purchases stay yours too, but the list of shop items you bought and event gifts you received is saved in the pet's iCloud share, so that the other person's phone shows only looks you own when it updates the pet's Live Activity for you (see Notifications).

Your name on the log is the one you set in Settings, or your first name in iCloud until you set one. Either of you can stop at any time: the owner keeps the pet, and the other person keeps a card of it on their own iPhone.

Friends

Friends are up to you too: someone becomes your friend only when you send them a link and say yes to who joined. There are no strangers, no public profiles and no chat. Every action is a preset (a wave, a snack, a visit, confetti), and the only words anyone types are names.

Each friendship lives in the inviter's iCloud, and Apple's CloudKit shares it with the friend (as with any iCloud share, CloudKit shows each of you the other's name from iCloud). A friend sees your pet card: your pet's name, your name (the one you set in Settings, or your first name in iCloud), your pet's line, form, LCD theme and shell color, a rough mood (happy, hungry, sad, sick or messy), whether it is asleep and until when, and whether it is on vacation or has flown home, plus its latest growing up. They also see your waves, gifts, visits and congratulations, and you see theirs; these are kept for 14 days.

Your coins, your pet's care log, its settings and your shop purchases stay yours. Remove a friend and the friendship's data goes from your devices; block them and their invitation links are refused from then on, on all your devices: the list of people you blocked is kept in your own iCloud too, and only you can read it.

Invite links and the App Clip

A friend invite link carries what a friend needs to see your pet before joining: the iCloud share link, your pet's name, its look (line, form, LCD theme and shell color), your display name and when the link was made. All of it is in the part of the link after the # sign, which a web browser never sends to a server.

The link points to a page on our relay server, which a browser shows if the app is not installed. That page is the same for everyone, and the relay never sees the part after the # sign.

The App Clip reads that part of the link on your iPhone to show the pet. The App Clip sends nothing to the developer.

The push relay

So that a partner's or friend's phone hears about your care or a wave within seconds, the app sends a short message to our relay server, which passes it on to Apple's push service. The message holds the push addresses Apple gave the recipient's devices; which shared pet or friendship it is about and what happened, and when (for example, that the pet was fed), with random ids for both; the names shown in the alert (such as the pet's name and your display name); for a Live Activity, how the pet is doing; a random number the app made for this iPhone; the app version; and a DeviceCheck token from Apple that shows the message comes from the real app.

The relay uses the push addresses and names only to build and send that push, and does not keep them. The push addresses reach your partner and friends through the iCloud share you have with them.

The relay writes nothing to disk and keeps no logs of push addresses, names, messages or IP addresses. To stop abuse, it counts recent requests in its memory, by IP address and by a one-way code made from each push address; these counts are never written down and are gone whenever the relay restarts. It also counts by the random number the app made for this iPhone, together with the IP address, and for a day it remembers a one-way code of each DeviceCheck token Apple has confirmed, so that a retried message is not checked twice. Its logs say only what kind of request and event came, how it went and how long it took.

The relay runs at Fly.io. If it is down, iCloud still delivers your changes, a little later.

Live play

During a visit with a friend, live play can send its messages (button presses, throws, waves and snacks) through the same relay, in real time. They are sealed on your phone with keys made from your friendship's secret, which the relay never has, so only your friend's phone can open them: they are encrypted end to end between the two phones. The relay passes the sealed messages on as they arrive and stores none of them.

To keep a visit's room from being reused, the relay remembers in its memory, for up to a day and only under a one-way code, that the room was used: when it began, how many times it was joined and how many messages passed, never what they said. Like the counts above, this is never written down and is gone whenever the relay restarts.

Game Center and SharePlay are Apple services, covered by Apple's privacy policy (apple.com/legal/privacy).

Nearby play and the Bump stay between the two phones: they talk directly to each other over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth, and the Bump measures the distance with Ultra Wideband. Nothing goes to a server.

Pet Studio

Pet Studio drawings and their names are kept on your devices and in your own iCloud (private database). When you hatch an egg with one, the pet carries a copy: the person you raise it with sees it, and so do your friends, on your pet card. Your partner's friends see the egg line's own adult on your partner's card; pictures either of you choose to share show your drawing. There is no public gallery.

Messages stickers

Stickers are made on your iPhone from your pets. A sticker is a picture of the pet (a pet hatched with a Studio drawing shows that drawing), and its description, which VoiceOver reads, names the pet. Messages, an Apple service, sends a sticker when you choose to send it, to the people you choose.

Notifications

Pet alerts are made on your iPhone, and alerts about your partner's and friends' pets are worded on it from what their phones send through our relay; no alert leaves your iPhone. The app registers with Apple's push service so iCloud can tell it when your pets or your friends' pets change on another device.

The Live Activity also gets push addresses from Apple's push service. Your phone's push addresses are saved in the iCloud share of each pet you raise together and of each friendship, with whether your alerts for it are on and make a sound, so that the other person's phones can ask our relay to send you their alerts and update a shared pet's Live Activity (see The push relay). In a pet you raise together, the same record also holds your iPhone's time zone and the list of shop items and event gifts you have; in a friendship, it holds when the pet on your card sleeps, in that pet's time zone, so that your friends' phones send you no alert while it sleeps.

Feedback

If you send feedback from Settings, the app opens an email to our support address with your app version, iOS version and device model written at the bottom, where you can read them before you send. We receive that email, with your email address and whatever you write, and use it only to answer you. Nothing else is added.

If you report someone, the app opens the same kind of email, with what you saw (their name, their pet's name and its Pet Studio form), the random id of your friendship or shared pet and, when the app knows it, the id iCloud gives that person in this app (the same on all their devices, never their name or Apple Account), so that we can look into it. We use it only to review the report: if it shows that they broke the Community Rules, we may add their id to the moderation list the app downloads (see Who receives your data), and the app then hides their names and drawings and removes them from friends and sharing. The list is public on our website and holds only those ids, never names.

Who receives your data

Apart from us, the person you raise a pet with and your friends, only these services receive data from Chirplet, and only what this policy describes: Adapty, which counts how the Premium paywalls are seen and bought (see Purchases); Meta Platforms, for our ads (see Our ads); Google, for Firebase Crashlytics and Google Analytics for Firebase (see Crash reports and Usage statistics); Fly.io, which runs our relay (see The push relay); GitHub, which hosts our website and the moderation list the app downloads (see below); and Apple, for App Store purchases, Apple Ads attribution (see Purchases), iCloud, push notifications, DeviceCheck, Game Center, SharePlay and Messages.

Each of these services is bound by its terms with us to give your data the same or equal protection as this policy and the App Store's rules require. If you allow tracking, Meta's own further use of the data it receives, such as for advertising on Facebook and Instagram, follows Meta's privacy policy instead (see Our ads). The app downloads the moderation list from our website on GitHub Pages to hide people removed for breaking the Community Rules (see Feedback); that request carries nothing about you beyond what every web request carries, such as your IP address, and, as for any site GitHub hosts, GitHub's own privacy statement applies to that request.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your pets and settings stay on your devices and in your iCloud until you delete them. Adapty keeps what it received as long as needed to provide purchases and support, then deletes it. Google keeps crash reports for 90 days and usage records for two months. Meta keeps what it received as Meta's privacy policy describes. The relay writes nothing down (see The push relay), and we keep feedback and report emails only as long as we need them to answer you or look into a report, except that an id on the moderation list, and the report that led to it, are kept as long as the removal stands (see Feedback).

Deleting the app deletes what it keeps on this iPhone; delete it from your Apple Watch too if it is there. iOS may keep one item after that: the random number the app made for this iPhone for the relay, in the iPhone's keychain, which says nothing about you. To delete the copy in your iCloud too, go to Settings, tap your name, then iCloud and the storage list, and delete Chirplet's data.

Deleting the app or its iCloud data does not delete what is not yours alone. What you added to a pet someone else owns or to a friendship someone else started (your name on the care log, your pet card, your waves and gifts, your push addresses) is in their iCloud and stays with them until they remove it; we cannot see or delete anything in anyone's iCloud. The person you raise a pet with and your friends keep what their phones already show, such as the card of a pet you raised together. Stickers you sent stay in Messages.

To have what Adapty, Google and Meta received about you, or any feedback or report email we hold from or about you, deleted, write to us at the support address at the top of this policy. We then ask Adapty to delete what it holds about your installation and purchases, Google to delete your installation's Firebase data (by its app instance id), and Meta to delete the events the app and Adapty sent about it; we delete those emails ourselves, except a report that keeps a removal on the moderation list in force; a person who was removed may write to us to ask us to review it. To withdraw your consent to tracking, turn it off in the iPhone's Settings, under Privacy and Security, then Tracking; to withdraw any other consent, write to us at the support address.

Children

Chirplet is not directed at children under 13 specifically. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children beyond the names and drawings they share with friends they have accepted, and a report someone sends about them (see Feedback). A parent who wants their child's data deleted can write to us at the support address.

Nothing else

Nothing is sold. Apart from what this policy describes, nothing leaves your iPhone.
